The race ratio Π: can maximum effort even keep pace?

Π compares your maximum output rate a with the effective drift of the field. Below the orange plane (Π < 1) the gap widens even at full effort, zero guilt and full capacity. The white line is the critical history length hc. Drag to rotate.

Π(h) = Π₀ / [1 + (1 + δA/gR)(egRh − 1)],   hc = (1/gR) ln[1 + (Π₀ − 1) gR/(gR + δA)]

05

How the wasted years erase the good future

The equilibria of the effort-year system as the length of history h grows. With a short history there are two futures, "catching up" and "the guilt trap", divided by a saddle. As the years pass, the good equilibrium and the saddle collide and disappear. This is a saddle-node bifurcation.

08

Something effort cannot touch

In matrix form, effort uA enters only the output equation. The controllability matrix has rank 1 < 2: no amount of effort changes the frontier R. The only lever is the horizon T. The feasibility ratio ρ(T) is the effort needed to reach parity, divided by your budget. Parity is reachable only if ρ(T) ≤ 1 at some T. The best horizon is near T* = 1/gR.

d/dt (A, R)ᵀ = [[−δA, 0],[0, gR]] (A, R)ᵀ + (b, 0)ᵀ uA + (0, ζ)ᵀ,   𝒞 = [B AB] = [[b, −δAb],[0, 0]]
09

What the model says

"It'll be impossible for the student to ever match the output to their input. But as long as they continue to go on without any guilt, they can work the best that day, and that's a great output toward that goal."

The maths agrees on both counts. Section 08 shows parity with the frontier may be unreachable. Sections 04 to 06 show that guilt is the one lever inside your control: the same hours give a smaller gap when G is low. The trap is a place in the flow, not a verdict.
